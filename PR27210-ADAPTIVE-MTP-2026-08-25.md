## The PP tax investigation (root cause found; long, worth reading)

Symptom: with the full PR patch, PP16384 collapsed 325 -> ~202 whenever MTP
spec was on (plain or adaptive; scaled with n_max: n1=271, n10=202; ngram-only
or spec-off unaffected: 296/369). TG unaffected (that is the PR's win).

Ruled out by controlled experiments (interleaved A/B, repeated runs, same
machine state; the user's iGPU game was identified as confounding several
absolute numbers incl. the 193.9 "clean" run):
- machine/clock state (my earlier "clock wedge" claim was wrong: I sampled
  clocks with no server running)
- GGML_CUDA_FA_ALL_QUANTS and all build flags (CMakeCache diffed vs proven build)
- stale incremental builds (samedir rebuild verified consistent with clean)
- my GDN snapshot-gating fix (interleaved: no effect)
- enum insertion position (moved to end: no effect)
- ctx-checkpoints, ctx size (190k same), VK1 cliff, ngram interplay, VRAM

Culprit (valid isolated test, 260.4 = parity): the PR's delta-net-base.cpp
conv-state hunk (`t_min = max(1, K - ubatch.n_seq_tokens + 1)` loop bound).
For 384-token PP ubatches the loop is literally identical to stock (t_min=1);
the only graphs it changes are small-ubatch ones (single-token decode: 1
conv-state CPY node instead of K=11). On this 3-GPU layer-split topology
that graph-shape change costs 38% of PP throughput with MTP on. Deeper
mechanism not chased - the hunk is a micro-optimization for other models'
verify paths and costs nothing to drop: reverting restores PP 326.4 while
keeping full adaptive MTP (TG 19.3).

Meta-lesson journaled: trusting "the diff cannot matter" over measurement
bit me repeatedly this session; the interleaved-A/B-with-repetition protocol
is what cracked it.
